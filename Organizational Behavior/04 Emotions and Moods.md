## 4 Emotions and Moods

## Key Points

#### 1. ⚡ Emotions and Feelings  
- Emotions are intense feelings triggered by specific events and involve chemical releases in the body lasting about six seconds.  
- It takes about 1/4 second for the brain to identify an emotional trigger and another 1/4 second to release emotion chemicals.  
- Feelings are the cognitive processing of emotions and last longer than emotions.  

#### 2. 🌥️ Moods and Affect  
- Moods are less intense than emotions, last from hours to days, and do not require a specific trigger.  
- Affect is a broad term that includes both emotions and moods.  

#### 3. ➕➖ Positive and Negative Affect  
- Positive affect includes emotions like joy, enthusiasm, and excitement; low positive affect includes boredom and fatigue.  
- Negative affect includes emotions like stress, anxiety, and nervousness; low negative affect includes calmness and serenity.  

#### 4. 🧠 Rationality and Emotions  
- Emotions are essential for rational thinking; brain injury studies show people need emotions to be rational.  
- Negative moods can improve the ability to recognize truthful information compared to happy moods.  

#### 5. ⚖️ Ethics and Emotions  
- Moral judgments are largely based on emotions such as sympathy, guilt, anger, and disgust rather than pure cognition.  

#### 6. 👤 Influences Based on Who You Are  
- Personality affects how frequently and intensely people experience emotions (affect intensity).  
- Older people experience fewer negative emotions and longer-lasting positive moods.  
- Women are generally more emotionally expressive and experience emotions more intensely than men, except for anger.  

#### 7. ⏰ Influences Based on Circumstances  
- Positive affect peaks in the evening and is lowest in the early morning; negative affect peaks overnight and is lowest later in the morning.  
- Positive affect is highest on Fridays, Saturdays, and Sundays, and lowest on Mondays.  
- Weather has little effect on most people's moods.  
- Stressful work events increase negative moods and build up over time.  
- Physical and social activities increase positive moods more than sedentary or formal events.  
- Poor sleep quality reduces job satisfaction and increases irritability.  
- Exercise improves positive mood, especially in depressed individuals.  
- Low blood sugar is linked to irritability and combative behavior; high blood sugar is linked to depression.  

#### 8. 😌 Emotional Labour  
- Emotional labour is the effort to display organizationally desired emotions during work interactions.  
- Felt emotions are actual emotions; displayed emotions are those required by the organization.  
- Surface acting involves faking emotions; deep acting involves trying to change true feelings.  
- Emotional dissonance occurs when there is a conflict between felt and displayed emotions.  

#### 9. 📊 Affective Events Theory (AET)  
- Employees’ emotional reactions to workplace events influence job performance and satisfaction.  
- People low in emotional stability react more strongly to negative events.  
- Emotions affect organizational citizenship behavior, commitment, effort, intention to quit, and workplace deviance.  

#### 10. 🌟 Emotional Intelligence (EQ)  
- EQ is the ability to perceive, understand, and regulate one’s own and others’ emotions.  
- High EQ improves teamwork, communication, leadership, conflict resolution, and overall workplace performance.  
- EQ is considered a skill or competency, not a personality trait.  

#### 11. 🛠️ Personal Emotion Regulation Strategies  
- Mindfulness meditation, cognitive reappraisal, journaling, deep breathing, exercise, social support, setting boundaries, self-compassion, problem-solving, and professional help improve emotional regulation.  

#### 12. 🏢 Organizational Strategies to Improve EQ  
- Training programs, coaching, feedback, open communication, mindfulness practices, team-building, leadership development, diversity initiatives, conflict resolution training, and regular assessments enhance emotional intelligence in organizations.

## Study Notes

### 1. 😊 Understanding Emotions and Moods: What Are They?

When we talk about emotions and moods, it’s important to first understand what these terms really mean and how they differ. Emotions are intense feelings triggered by specific events or people. From a biological perspective, emotions are chemicals released in our bodies in response to how we interpret a situation. This process happens incredibly fast—our brain identifies the trigger in about a quarter of a second, and then another quarter of a second to release these chemicals. These chemicals don’t just stay in the brain; they circulate throughout the body, creating a feedback loop between our brain and body that lasts roughly six seconds.

Feelings, on the other hand, are what we experience as we start to process and think about these emotions. Unlike emotions, which are brief and intense, feelings are more “cognitively saturated,” meaning they involve more thought and reflection. Feelings often last longer than emotions because they are a mix of several emotional responses.

Moods are different from both emotions and feelings. A mood is a more general state of mind that is less intense than an emotion and doesn’t always have a clear cause. Moods can last for hours or even days and are influenced by many factors like the environment (weather, lighting), our physical state (health, exercise), and our mental focus. Finally, the term “affect” is a broad word that covers all kinds of feelings, including both emotions and moods.

#### Summary:
- **Emotions:** Intense, short-lived chemical responses to specific triggers.
- **Feelings:** Longer-lasting, thought-processed experiences of emotions.
- **Moods:** Less intense, longer-lasting states influenced by many factors.
- **Affect:** A general term for all feelings, including emotions and moods.


### 2. ⚖️ Positive and Negative Affect: The Basic Moods

Moods can be broadly categorized into two types: positive affect and negative affect. These categories help us understand the general emotional tone or mood someone is experiencing.

- **Positive Affect** refers to moods that include feelings like excitement, enthusiasm, and joy. When someone has a high positive affect, they feel energetic and happy. On the other hand, low positive affect means feelings like boredom, fatigue, or depression—basically, a lack of positive emotions.

- **Negative Affect** includes feelings such as nervousness, stress, and anxiety. High negative affect means someone is feeling tense or upset, while low negative affect means they feel calm, content, or serene.

These two mood dimensions help explain how people generally feel over time and can influence their behavior and performance.


### 3. 🧠 The Role of Emotions and Moods in Our Lives and Work

Emotions and moods are not just random feelings; they play important roles in how we think, behave, and interact, especially in the workplace.

#### Rationality and Emotions
Many people believe that emotions and rational thinking are opposites—that if you show emotion, you’re being irrational. However, research shows the opposite: emotions are essential for rational thinking. For example, studies of people with brain injuries reveal that without emotions, people struggle to make rational decisions. Emotions provide context and meaning to the information we receive, helping us understand the world better. Interestingly, people in a negative mood may even be better at recognizing truthful information than those in a happy mood.

#### Ethics and Emotions
Emotions also influence our moral judgments. Feelings like sympathy, guilt, anger, and disgust guide how we perceive right and wrong. While we often think of moral decisions as logical, they are deeply connected to our emotional responses.


### 4. 🌱 What Influences Our Emotions and Moods?

Our emotions and moods are shaped by two main types of influences: who we are as individuals and the circumstances we find ourselves in.

#### Influences Based on Who You Are
- **Personality:** Some people naturally experience emotions more intensely than others. This is called affect intensity. For example, affectively intense people feel both happiness and sadness more deeply.
- **Age:** Research shows that as people get older, they tend to experience fewer negative emotions and their positive moods last longer.
- **Gender:** Women generally express emotions more openly and intensely than men, except for anger, which men tend to express more.

#### Influences Based on Your Circumstances
- **Time of Day:** Positive moods tend to be highest in the evening and lowest in the early morning. Negative moods peak overnight and are lowest later in the morning.
- **Day of the Week:** People usually feel happiest on weekends and lowest on Mondays.
- **Weather:** Surprisingly, weather has little effect on most people’s moods.
- **Stress:** Stressful events at work or in life can build up and increase negative emotions.
- **Social Activities:** Physical and social activities like hiking or eating with friends boost positive moods more than sedentary or formal events.
- **Sleep:** Poor sleep quality leads to fatigue, irritability, and lower job satisfaction.
- **Exercise:** Regular exercise improves mood, especially for those who are depressed.
- **Blood Sugar Levels:** Low blood sugar can cause irritability and aggressive behavior, while very high blood sugar is linked to depression.


### 5. 😌 Emotional Labour: Managing Emotions at Work

Emotional labour refers to the effort employees put into managing their emotions to meet the expectations of their job. This is especially important in roles that require interaction with customers or clients.

- **Felt Emotions:** These are the real emotions a person actually feels.
- **Displayed Emotions:** These are the emotions the organization expects employees to show, which may not match their true feelings.

When employees have to show emotions they don’t feel, they engage in **surface acting**—pretending to feel something they don’t, like smiling when unhappy. **Deep acting** is when employees try to change their actual feelings to match what they need to display.

When there’s a big difference between felt and displayed emotions, it causes **emotional dissonance**, which can be stressful and exhausting.


### 6. 📊 Affective Events Theory (AET): How Work Events Shape Emotions

Affective Events Theory explains how specific events at work trigger emotional reactions, which then influence job performance and satisfaction. For example, receiving praise might boost positive emotions and motivation, while a reprimand might cause negative feelings and reduce commitment.

People who are less emotionally stable tend to react more strongly to negative events. Also, the mood someone is already in can affect how they respond emotionally to new events.

Emotions at work influence many important outcomes, such as:
- How much effort employees put in
- Their commitment to the organization
- Whether they engage in helpful behaviors beyond their job (organizational citizenship)
- Their intention to quit
- Incidences of workplace deviance or misconduct


### 7. 🌟 Emotional Intelligence (EQ): Understanding and Managing Emotions

Emotional Intelligence (EQ) is the ability to recognize, understand, and manage your own emotions and the emotions of others. Unlike IQ, which measures cognitive intelligence, EQ focuses on emotional skills that are crucial for success in personal and professional life.

#### What is Emotional Intelligence?
- **Perceiving Emotions:** Being aware of your own and others’ emotions.
- **Understanding Emotions:** Knowing what emotions mean and how they influence behavior.
- **Regulating Emotions:** Managing your emotional responses effectively.

#### Why is EQ Important at Work?
High emotional intelligence helps improve teamwork, communication, leadership, and conflict resolution. It creates a positive work environment and boosts productivity and employee satisfaction.

#### How to Improve Emotional Intelligence
- **Personal Strategies:** Mindfulness meditation, journaling, deep breathing, exercise, social support, setting boundaries, self-compassion, problem-solving, and seeking professional help.
- **Organizational Strategies:** Training programs, coaching, feedback systems, open communication, mindfulness practices, team-building, leadership development, diversity initiatives, conflict resolution training, and regular assessments.

People with high EQ can express themselves clearly, relate well to others, and handle daily challenges with optimism and resilience.


#### Final Thoughts

Understanding emotions and moods is essential not only for personal well-being but also for creating effective and harmonious workplaces. Emotions influence how we think, behave, and interact with others. By recognizing the role of emotions, managing emotional labour, and developing emotional intelligence, individuals and organizations can improve performance, satisfaction, and overall success.
