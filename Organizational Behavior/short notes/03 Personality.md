# Organizational Behaviour - Lesson 3: Personality

## Personality and Its Determinants
- **Personality** consists of relatively stable characteristics and traits that help explain why a person behaves in a particular way.
- **Hereditary factors** are determined at conception and include influences on physical features, temperament, energy level, reflexes, and biological rhythms.
- The hereditary approach links personality to genetic, physiological, and psychological makeup, but **personality can change across a lifetime**.
- Personality tends to be more changeable in adolescence and more stable in adulthood.
- **Personality traits** are characteristics that appear consistently across many situations.
  - The more consistent a characteristic is over time and across different situations, the more important it is in describing the individual.

## Dominant Personality Frameworks
### Myers-Briggs Type Indicator
- The **Myers-Briggs Type Indicator (MBTI)** is a personality assessment that classifies people using four pairs of preferences.
  - **Extraverted versus introverted** - outgoing, sociable, and assertive versus quiet and reserved.
  - **Sensing versus intuitive** - practical, orderly, and detail-focused versus focused on the big picture and unconscious processes.
  - **Thinking versus feeling** - decisions based mainly on reason and logic versus personal values and emotions.
  - **Judging versus perceiving** - preference for order and structure versus flexibility and spontaneity.
- Combining one preference from each pair produces **16 personality types**.
- A criticism of the MBTI is that its theoretical framework **treats personality as static** and does not sufficiently allow for development and change over time.

### Big Five Model
- The **Big Five Model** has strong links with important organizational behaviour outcomes.
- **Extraversion** reflects comfort with relationships and stimulation.
  - High scorers tend to be sociable, assertive, and energized by busy environments; low scorers tend to be reserved and prefer less stimulation.
- **Agreeableness** reflects the tendency to cooperate and defer to others.
  - High scorers tend to be warm, trusting, and cooperative; low scorers tend to be antagonistic and disagreeable.
- **Conscientiousness** reflects reliability.
  - High scorers tend to be responsible, organized, dependable, and persistent; low scorers tend to be distracted, disorganized, and unreliable.
- **Emotional stability** reflects the ability to withstand stress and is the opposite of neuroticism.
  - High emotional stability is associated with calmness, confidence, and security; low stability with nervousness, anxiety, depression, and insecurity.
- **Openness to experience** reflects interest in novelty.
  - High scorers tend to be creative, curious, and artistically sensitive; low scorers tend to prefer familiar and conventional experiences.

## Dark Triad Traits
- The **Dark Triad** consists of Machiavellianism, narcissism, and psychopathy, three socially undesirable traits with workplace implications.
- **Machiavellianism** describes a pragmatic, emotionally distant approach in which a person may believe that ends justify means.
  - High Machiavellianism is associated with manipulation, aggression, and counterproductive work behaviour; short-term gains can be lost because others dislike or distrust the person.
- **Narcissism** involves grandiose self-importance, a need for admiration, entitlement, and arrogance.
  - Narcissists may appear charismatic and adaptable, and some evidence in the slides links narcissism with leadership and complex decision making.
- **Psychopathy** in organizational behaviour refers to lack of concern for others and lack of guilt or remorse when actions cause harm.
  - It is associated with deceit, impulsivity, low empathy, hard influence tactics, manipulation, and bullying.
  - **Predatory bullying** is tormenting vulnerable people for its own sake; **instrumental bullying** is used to achieve goals such as power or promotion.
  - Psychopathic traits may help a person gain power but may prevent that power from being used toward healthy organizational ends.

## Personality and Situations
- Personality does not determine behaviour by itself because **the effect of a trait depends on the situation**.
- **Situation strength** is the degree to which norms, cues, or standards clearly dictate appropriate behaviour.
  - Strong situations specify expected behaviour and discourage alternatives, so personality predicts behaviour less strongly.
  - Weak situations provide more freedom, so personality traits and personal values have more opportunity to influence behaviour.
- Very strong organizational situations can be **dull or demotivating, unsuitable for individual differences, and restrictive of creativity and initiative**.
- **Trait Activation Theory** proposes that particular situations activate some traits more strongly than others.
  - A commission-based reward system can activate differences in extraversion because extraversion is sensitive to rewards.
  - Personality differences matter more when the environment does not already strongly push everyone toward the same behaviour.

## Values
- **Values** are basic beliefs that a particular mode of conduct or end state is personally or socially preferable to its opposite.
- People enter organizations with beliefs about what **ought and ought not to be**, including judgments of right, wrong, and preferred outcomes.
- Values can clarify decisions or bias judgment, but they influence **attitudes and workplace behaviour** either way.
- Rokeach separates values into two categories:
  - **Terminal values** - desirable end states or lifetime goals, such as economic success and prosperity.
  - **Instrumental values** - preferred ways of behaving or means of reaching terminal values, such as discipline and being goal-oriented.
- Effective behaviour requires attention to both the **ends and the means** used to reach them.

## Values and Fit at Work
- Organizations increasingly consider how well a person's **personality and values fit the job and the organization**, not only whether the person can perform a specific task.
- **Person-job fit** is described by John Holland's theory, which links satisfaction and turnover to the match between personality and occupation.
  - **Realistic** - prefers physical activities requiring skill, strength, and coordination.
  - **Investigative** - prefers thinking, organizing, and understanding.
  - **Social** - prefers helping and developing others.
  - **Conventional** - prefers rule-regulated, orderly, and unambiguous activities.
  - **Enterprising** - prefers influencing others and attaining power.
  - **Artistic** - prefers ambiguous, unsystematic activities that allow creative expression.
- Holland's types form a **hexagon of compatibility** - adjacent types are more similar, while diagonally opposite types are more dissimilar.
- Personality-job agreement is associated with **higher satisfaction and lower turnover**.
- **Person-organization fit** proposes that people are attracted to and selected by organizations whose values match their own and tend to leave organizations that are incompatible with them.
  - Better fit with organizational culture should increase satisfaction and reduce turnover.

## International Values
- Unlike personality, values are **learned from the environment, shared with others, and passed between generations**, so national cultures develop different value systems.
- **Hofstede's framework** compares work-related values across national cultures; the original study surveyed more than 116,000 IBM employees in 40 countries.
- The cultural dimensions shown in the lecture include:
  - **Power distance** - acceptance of unequal distribution of power, from egalitarian to strongly hierarchical.
  - **Individualism versus collectivism** - priority given to individual goals versus group goals.
  - **Uncertainty avoidance** - comfort or discomfort with unknown and uncertain situations.
  - **Masculinity versus femininity** - emphasis on achievement and power versus nurturing and quality of life.
  - **Long-term orientation** - emphasis on future and long-term goals versus tradition and short-term goals.
  - **Restraint versus indulgence** - control of desires and impulses versus allowing their satisfaction.
