# Organizational Behaviour - Lesson 1: Introduction

## Meaning and Functions of Work
- **Work** is an activity that produces something of value for other people.
- Work serves an **economic function** by providing income and also serves social functions through interaction with others.
- Work can provide **social status, identity, self-esteem, and self-actualisation**.
- Work contributes to **self-esteem** in two main ways:
  - It lets people demonstrate competence and mastery over themselves and their environment.
  - It shows people that they can produce something valuable and have something significant to offer.
- **Alienation** occurs when work fails to provide these functions, producing feelings of powerlessness, meaninglessness, and normlessness.

## Organizational Behaviour
- **Organizational behaviour (OB)** studies how individuals, groups, and organizational structure affect behaviour within organizations, with the aim of improving organizational effectiveness.
- In simple terms, OB studies **what people do in organizations and how their behaviour affects performance**.
- OB aims to **understand and predict human behaviour** in organizations.
- Human behaviour is difficult to predict but is not random because underlying consistencies exist.
- OB studies behaviour at three main levels:
  - **Individual** - characteristics and behaviour of individual employees.
  - **Group** - interactions, relationships, and group processes.
  - **Organization** - structure, systems, and organization-wide influences.
- Core OB topics include **motivation, leadership and power, communication, group processes, attitudes and perception, change, conflict and negotiation, and work design**.

## Multidisciplinary Nature of OB
- OB is an **applied behavioural science** drawing mainly from psychology, social psychology, sociology, anthropology, political science, and neuroscience.
- **Psychology** studies and seeks to explain or change individual behaviour, including learning, motivation, personality, perception, emotions, job satisfaction, decision making, leadership, selection, work design, and stress.
- **Social psychology** connects psychology and sociology and focuses on how groups influence individuals, including attitude change, communication, group processes, and group decisions.
- **Sociology** examines how society and social systems shape people, contributing to the study of communication, power, conflict, organizational structure, technology, change, and culture.
- **Anthropology** examines cultures, values, rituals, and cross-cultural differences that shape organizations.
- **Political science** contributes to understanding power, conflict, ethics, communication, and intergroup behaviour.
- **Neuroscience** contributes to understanding perception, attribution, learning, and motivation.

## Beginnings of OB and the Hawthorne Studies
- OB developed as a distinct discipline when scientific approaches to management showed that **behavioural factors can predict productivity**.
- The **Hawthorne Studies** were conducted from 1924 to 1932 at Western Electric's Hawthorne plant and are considered a milestone in management science.
- The original interpretation was that **positive managerial treatment and human relations increased employee output**, rather than money being the main cause.
- Later analysis suggested that **money also mattered**, while productivity was also affected by employees feeling important and developing group pride from being selected for the study.
- The **Hawthorne effect** is the tendency for subjects to behave differently because researchers are actively observing or involving them in a study.

## Contingency in OB
- Unlike physical sciences, OB has **few simple absolutes** because people differ and human behaviour is complex.
- OB therefore uses a **contingency approach** - a relationship such as x leading to y may hold only under particular conditions represented by z.
- General OB concepts must be applied to the **specific situation, person, or group** rather than treated as universal rules.

## The Organizational Iceberg
- The **organizational iceberg** shows that visible formal features are only a small part of an organization.
- **Overt formal aspects** include goals, financial resources, technology, physical facilities, customers, organization design, rules, and visible skills.
- **Covert behavioural aspects** include attitudes, communication patterns, informal team processes, personality, conflict, political behaviour, and underlying skills.
- Hidden behavioural factors can strongly affect **productivity and quality of working life**, so managers must look beyond formal structures.
- An organization is not a single tangible object but an **abstract and ambiguous system of relationships, behaviour, structures, and processes**.

## Management as an Integrating Activity
- **Management coordinates, directs, and guides** the efforts of organizational members toward organizational goals.
- People are not owned like physical resources; they bring their own **perceptions, feelings, attitudes, values, abilities, and expectations** to work.
- Behaviour in organizations is influenced by the **individual, group, organization, and external environment**.
- Management integrates organizational processes, employee effort, work activities, motivation, job satisfaction, and rewards to improve the **people-organization relationship**.
- Effective management seeks to **satisfy employee needs, create a climate where people work willingly and effectively, and achieve organizational goals**.
- Successful management therefore requires understanding both **human personality and work organizations**.

## Implications for Managers
- Use **scientifically validated information** about human behaviour to improve interpersonal and leadership skills.
- Use **evidence, metrics, and situational variables rather than hunches** when explaining cause-and-effect relationships.
- Continue developing technical and conceptual skills and remain current with new OB practices and trends.
- OB can help managers improve quality and productivity through **employee empowerment, change programs, customer service, and management of work-life conflicts**.
- Apply OB research cautiously across countries because **cultural norms and expectations can change how findings apply**.
- Consider relevant factors at the **individual, group, and organizational levels together** rather than focusing on only one level.
