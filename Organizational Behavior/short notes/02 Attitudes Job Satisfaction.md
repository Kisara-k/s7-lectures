# Organizational Behaviour - Lesson 2: Attitudes and Job Satisfaction

## Recognition of Individuality

* The **individual is a central feature of organizational behaviour** because organizations are made up of people acting alone and in groups.
* Individual behaviour is shaped by **organizational expectations and the external environment**.
* **Individual differences** can create creativity, enjoyment, and satisfaction, but can also create conflict and frustration.
* Managers need to select and develop people effectively and understand **their own personality and impact on others**.
* Organizational and environmental change increases pressure on employees, so managers need sensitivity to **new attitudes, perceptions, strain, and stress**.
* Individuals differ in culture, family experience, motivation, attitudes, personality, intelligence, abilities, perception, and other characteristics.

## Attitudes

* **Attitude** is how a person views and evaluates an idea, object, person, or situation and reflects a tendency to respond positively or negatively.
* Three major explanations describe how attitudes form:

  * **Dispositional approach** - attitudes are relatively stable predispositions, almost like personality traits, so some people tend to remain satisfied or dissatisfied across jobs.
  * **Situational approach** - attitudes develop from the particular situation and can change with work conditions, supervisors, rewards, and other experiences.
  * **Social-information-processing approach** - social context shapes the information and reality people perceive, which then shapes their attitudes.
* Research suggests **dispositions and socially constructed realities interact** rather than one explanation being sufficient by itself.

## Beliefs, Values, and Attitude Components

* **Beliefs** concern what a person understands to be true about the world - what "is."
* **Values** concern what a person believes is desirable or what "should" be.
* Attitudes have three closely related components:

  * **Cognition** - beliefs or evaluations about how things are, such as "My pay is low."
  * **Affect** - emotional or feeling responses, such as anger about low pay.
  * **Behaviour** - an intention to act in a particular way, such as looking for another job.
* Cognition, affect, and behaviour are **closely interconnected and difficult to separate** in practice.
* People may not fully understand their own attitudes, so their explanations for their behaviour can sometimes be inaccurate.
* Observable **behaviour is only the visible part**; attitudes, values, motives, ethics, beliefs, standards, and judgments can lie beneath it.

## Behavioural Justification

* People seek **cognitive consistency**, meaning they prefer their behaviour and attitudes to fit together.
* **Cognitive dissonance** is perceived incompatibility between attitudes or between behaviour and attitudes.
* Dissonance creates tension, so people try to **reduce the inconsistency and restore consistency**.

## Major Job Attitudes

* The most widely studied job attitudes are **job satisfaction, job involvement, and organizational commitment**.
* Other important attitudes include **perceived organizational support, employee engagement, and organizational citizenship behaviour**.

## Job Satisfaction

* **Job satisfaction** is a positive feeling about a job resulting from an evaluation of its characteristics.
* Overall satisfaction is a **combined evaluation of many job elements**, including coworkers, supervisors, rules, performance standards, and working conditions.
* Job satisfaction is an **emotional response to a job situation** and can be understood through the discrepancy between expectations and outcomes.

  * Outcomes that meet or exceed expectations tend to increase satisfaction and desire to remain.
  * Outcomes below expectations tend to increase dissatisfaction.
* Five important dimensions shape job satisfaction:

  * **Work itself** - interesting tasks, learning opportunities, and responsibility.
  * **Pay** - amount, perceived fairness, and method of payment.
  * **Promotional opportunities** - realistic chances for advancement.
  * **Supervision** - supervisors' ability and consideration for employees.
  * **Coworkers** - friendliness, competence, and support.

## Measuring Job Satisfaction

* Managers often monitor satisfaction as an indicator of **organizational effectiveness**.
* The most common method is a **rating scale**, such as agreement ratings from strongly disagree to strongly agree.
* Rating scales assume employees are **willing and able to report feelings accurately** and that questions **mean the same thing to everyone**.

## Responses to Dissatisfaction

* The **exit-voice-loyalty-neglect framework** describes four responses:

  * **Voice** - active and constructive attempts to improve conditions.
  * **Exit** - active and destructive withdrawal from the organization.
  * **Loyalty** - passive and constructive waiting or support while conditions improve.
  * **Neglect** - passive and destructive withdrawal of effort or attention.

## Other Job Attitudes

### Job Involvement and Empowerment

* **Job involvement** is the extent to which a person is interested in, identifies with, and is committed to assigned work.
* **Psychological empowerment** is belief in one's influence over the work environment, competence, meaningful work, and autonomy.

### Organizational Commitment

* **Organizational commitment** is the strength of a person's identification with and involvement in an organization.
* Strong commitment includes belief in organizational goals and values, willingness to exert effort, and a **strong desire to remain a member**.
* Commitment goes beyond passive loyalty; **emotional attachment and belief in organizational values** are especially important.

### Perceived Organizational Support

* **Perceived organizational support (POS)** is the belief that the organization values employee contributions and cares about employee well-being.
* POS increases when **rewards are fair, employees have a voice, and supervisors are supportive**.
* Strong POS is associated with more citizenship behaviour, less tardiness, and better customer service.

### Employee Engagement

* **Employee engagement** is involvement with, satisfaction with, and enthusiasm for one's work.
* Highly engaged employees show passion and connection, while disengaged employees may contribute time without much energy or attention.
* The slides report that only **17% to 29% of employees are highly engaged**, making engagement an organizational concern.

### Organizational Citizenship Behaviour

* **Organizational citizenship behaviours (OCBs)** are discretionary actions outside the formal job description, such as choosing to go the extra mile.
* Positive OCBs can reduce supervision needs, improve morale, and produce cost-saving suggestions, but excessive extra-role effort can contribute to **burnout**.
* Job attitudes are **not completely distinct** - they overlap substantially because common personality and workplace factors can influence several attitudes at once.
