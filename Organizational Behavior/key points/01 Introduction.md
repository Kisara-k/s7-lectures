## 1 Introduction

## Key Points

#### 1. ⚙️ Meaning and Functions of Work  
- Work is an activity that produces something of value for other people.  
- Work serves economic, social, identity, self-esteem, and self-actualization functions.  
- Work contributes to self-esteem by allowing individuals to demonstrate competence and produce value.  
- Alienation occurs when work fails to provide meaning, leading to feelings of powerlessness and meaninglessness.  

#### 2. 📚 Definition of Organizational Behaviour (OB)  
- OB studies the impact of individuals, groups, and structure on behaviour within organizations.  
- The goal of OB is to understand and predict human behaviour to improve organizational effectiveness.  
- OB focuses on three determinants of behaviour: individuals, groups, and organizational structure.  

#### 3. 📋 Core Topics of OB  
- Motivation  
- Leader behaviour and power  
- Interpersonal communication  
- Group structure and processes  
- Attitude development and perception  
- Change processes  
- Conflict and negotiation  
- Work design  

#### 4. 🌐 Multidisciplinary Nature of OB  
- OB is based mainly on psychology, social psychology, sociology, anthropology, political science, and neuroscience.  
- Psychology is the most important contributor to OB, focusing on individual behaviour.  
- Sociology studies how society shapes people and social contexts.  
- Social psychology studies the impact of groups on individuals.  
- Anthropology examines cultural rites and rituals in organizations.  
- Political science studies power dynamics within organizations.  

#### 5. 🔍 Hawthorne Studies  
- Conducted at Western Electric’s Hawthorne plant in the 1920s-30s by Elton Mayo and Fritz Roethlisberger.  
- Found that money was not the sole cause of increased productivity.  
- Positive treatment by managers improved employee performance (human relations).  
- Worker productivity increased due to feelings of importance and group pride.  
- The Hawthorne effect: subjects change behaviour because they know they are being observed.  

#### 6. ❗ Absolutes and Contingencies in OB  
- OB has no universal laws like physical sciences due to human complexity.  
- OB concepts depend on situational or contingency variables.  
- Behavioural outcomes (X leads to Y) occur only under specific conditions (Z).  

#### 7. 🧊 Organizational Iceberg Concept  
- Organizations have visible (formal structures, policies) and invisible (behavioural, cultural) aspects.  
- The invisible aspects (attitudes, informal networks, norms) significantly affect productivity and work quality.  
- Organizations are intangible and complex, making them difficult to fully understand or manage.  

#### 8. 🤝 Management as an Integrating Activity  
- Management coordinates and directs individual and group efforts toward organizational goals.  
- People are not owned resources; they bring their own perceptions, feelings, and attitudes.  
- Successful management requires integrating individual personalities with organizational needs.  

#### 9. 📈 Implications for Managers  
- Use scientifically validated information to improve interpersonal skills and leadership.  
- Rely on metrics and situational variables rather than hunches for cause-effect explanations.  
- Stay current with OB trends like big data and improve technical and conceptual skills.  
- Empower employees, design effective change programs, and help balance work-life conflicts.  
- Be cautious applying OB research internationally due to cultural differences.  
- Consider individual, group, and organizational levels to improve management effectiveness.
