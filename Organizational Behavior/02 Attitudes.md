## 2 Attitudes

## Key Points

#### 1. 🧍 Recognition of Individuality  
- Organizations are made up of individual members whose unique traits influence organizational behavior.  
- Individual differences can foster creativity and satisfaction but also cause conflict and frustration.  
- Managers must understand their own personality and its impact on others to manage relationships effectively.  
- Individuals differ in ethnic origin, gender, family background, culture, motivation, personality, intelligence, and perception.

#### 2. 🧠 Attitudes  
- Attitude is a person’s predisposition to respond positively or negatively toward an idea, object, person, or situation.  
- Attitudes can be formed through three approaches: dispositional, situational, and social-information-processing.  
- Dispositional approach views attitudes as stable personality traits.  
- Situational approach sees attitudes as responses to specific work conditions.  
- Social-information-processing approach states attitudes are shaped by social context and perceptions.  
- Both dispositional and social-information-processing approaches interact to form attitudes.

#### 3. 💭 Beliefs and Values  
- Beliefs are about what is known or perceived as reality ("what is").  
- Values are about what is desirable or what "should" be.

#### 4. 🧩 Main Components of Attitude  
- Cognition: beliefs or thoughts about something (e.g., “My pay is low”).  
- Affect: emotional feelings toward something (e.g., “I am angry about my pay”).  
- Behavior: intention to act in a certain way (e.g., “I will look for another job”).  
- These components are closely related and often inseparable.

#### 5. ⚖️ Behavioural Justification and Cognitive Dissonance  
- Cognitive dissonance is the tension from inconsistency between attitudes and behavior.  
- People seek behavioral justification to reduce this tension and restore cognitive consistency.

#### 6. 💼 Major Job Attitudes  
- Key job attitudes include job satisfaction, job involvement, and organizational commitment.  
- Other important attitudes: perceived organizational support, employee engagement, and organizational citizenship behavior (OCB).

#### 7. 😊 Job Satisfaction  
- Job satisfaction is a positive emotional response based on evaluation of job characteristics.  
- Satisfaction depends on the discrepancy between job expectations and outcomes.  
- Five key dimensions affect job satisfaction: work itself, pay, promotional opportunities, supervision, and coworkers.  
- Job satisfaction is commonly measured using rating scales.  
- Dissatisfied employees may respond by exit, voice, loyalty, or neglect.

#### 8. 🔄 Other Job Attitudes  
- Job involvement: degree of identification and commitment to work tasks.  
- Psychological empowerment: belief in influence, competence, meaningfulness, and autonomy at work.  
- Organizational commitment: emotional attachment, acceptance of goals, effort willingness, and desire to stay.  
- Perceived organizational support (POS): belief that the organization values contributions and cares for well-being.  
- Employee engagement: involvement, satisfaction, and enthusiasm for work; only 17-29% of employees are highly engaged.  
- Organizational citizenship behavior (OCB): voluntary, discretionary behaviors beyond formal job duties that benefit the organization.

#### 9. 🔍 Overlap of Job Attitudes  
- Job attitudes like satisfaction, involvement, and commitment overlap significantly, influenced by personality and interrelations.

## Study Notes

### 1. 🧍 Recognition of Individuality: Understanding the Unique Person in Organizations

In any organization, the individual is the fundamental building block. Organizations are not just abstract entities; they are made up of people—each with their own unique traits, backgrounds, and ways of thinking. Recognizing individuality means appreciating that every employee brings something different to the table, whether they are working alone or as part of a team. This uniqueness influences how they respond to the organization’s expectations and the external environment.

Individual differences can be a double-edged sword. On the positive side, they can spark creativity, enjoyment, and satisfaction at work. On the downside, these differences can also lead to misunderstandings, conflicts, or frustration if not managed well. Therefore, managers must be skilled in selecting and developing individuals who will add value to the organization.

Moreover, managers need to understand themselves—their own personalities and how these affect others. Managing relationships at work is a critical skill, especially today when rapid changes in organizations and the external environment put extra pressure on employees. When changes feel forced or imposed from outside, managing people requires even greater sensitivity. Such changes often lead to new mindsets, attitudes, and perceptions that help people adapt to the evolving workplace.

Managers must also be aware of the stress employees experience during times of change, while managing their own stress levels. People differ in many ways, including ethnic origin, gender, early family experiences, social and cultural background, motivation, personality traits, intelligence, and perception. All these factors shape how individuals behave and interact at work.


### 2. 🧠 Attitudes: How We See and React to the World Around Us

Attitudes are essentially how a person views and evaluates something—whether it’s a person, an idea, an object, or a situation. When someone says, “I like my job,” they are expressing their attitude toward work. Attitudes are important because they influence how people behave and respond in various situations.

#### How Are Attitudes Formed?

There are three main ways psychologists explain how attitudes develop:

1. **Dispositional Approach:** This view suggests that attitudes are stable traits, almost like part of a person’s personality. For example, some people tend to be happy at work no matter what the job is like, while others tend to be unhappy regardless of the situation. Research shows that people’s attitudes often don’t change much even when they switch jobs, supporting this idea.

2. **Situational Approach:** This approach argues that attitudes depend on the specific situation a person is in. For example, if someone has a boring job or a bad boss, they might develop negative attitudes because of those experiences. So, attitudes can change as work conditions change.

3. **Social-Information-Processing Approach:** According to this view, attitudes are shaped by the social context—the “socially constructed realities” around a person. How someone perceives their environment and the people in it influences their attitudes.

In reality, all these approaches have some truth. People’s attitudes are shaped by a mix of their personality traits and the social environment they are in.


### 3. 💭 Beliefs and Values: What We Know and What We Care About

Beliefs and values are closely related but different concepts. **Beliefs** are about what we think is true or real—our understanding of how the world works. For example, believing that hard work leads to success is a belief.

**Values**, on the other hand, are about what we think *should* be—what is important or desirable. Values guide our judgments about right and wrong, good and bad. For example, valuing honesty means you believe being truthful is important.

Together, beliefs and values shape our attitudes and behaviors.


### 4. 🧩 Main Components of Attitude: What Makes Up an Attitude?

Attitudes are made up of three interconnected parts:

- **Cognition (Thoughts):** This is what we believe or think about something. For example, “My pay is low” is a cognitive statement about a job.

- **Affect (Feelings):** This is the emotional part of an attitude. For example, “I am angry about how little I’m paid” expresses feelings.

- **Behavior (Actions):** This is how we intend to act based on our attitude. For example, “I’m going to look for another job” shows a behavioral intention.

These components are closely linked. Often, our thoughts influence our feelings, which then influence our actions. But in reality, these parts are hard to separate, and sometimes we don’t fully understand why we feel or act a certain way.

For example, if an employee misses a promotion they expected, their attitude might be: they believe they deserved it (cognition), feel upset about their supervisor (affect), and complain or look for another job (behavior).


### 5. ⚖️ Behavioural Justification and Cognitive Dissonance: Aligning Actions and Attitudes

People generally want their behaviors to match their attitudes. When there’s a mismatch—called **cognitive dissonance**—it creates psychological discomfort or tension. For example, if someone acts in a way that conflicts with their beliefs, they feel uneasy.

To reduce this tension, people try to justify their behavior or change their attitudes to restore consistency. This process is called **behavioural justification**. It helps maintain a sense of internal harmony and makes people feel more comfortable with their actions.


### 6. 💼 Major Job Attitudes: How Employees Feel About Their Work

Employees hold many attitudes about their jobs, but some are especially important:

- **Job Satisfaction:** How positively or negatively a person feels about their job overall.
- **Job Involvement:** How much a person identifies with and cares about their work.
- **Organizational Commitment:** How strongly a person feels connected to and loyal toward their organization.

Other important attitudes include:

- **Perceived Organizational Support:** How much employees believe their organization values and supports them.
- **Employee Engagement:** The level of enthusiasm and passion employees have for their work.
- **Organizational Citizenship Behaviour (OCB):** Voluntary actions employees take that go beyond their job requirements to help the organization.


### 7. 😊 Job Satisfaction: What Makes People Happy at Work?

Job satisfaction is a positive emotional response to one’s job. It’s not just about liking the work itself but also about how well the job meets a person’s expectations. If the outcomes of a job (like pay, work conditions, or recognition) meet or exceed what the employee expects, they tend to be satisfied.

#### Key Dimensions of Job Satisfaction

Research identifies five main areas that influence job satisfaction:

1. **Work Itself:** How interesting and challenging the tasks are, and whether they offer learning and responsibility.
2. **Pay:** The amount of pay, fairness of pay, and how it is delivered.
3. **Promotional Opportunities:** Chances for advancement within the organization.
4. **Supervision:** The skills and supportiveness of supervisors.
5. **Coworkers:** The friendliness, competence, and supportiveness of colleagues.

#### Measuring Job Satisfaction

Managers often use rating scales or surveys to measure job satisfaction because it helps them understand how employees feel about their work and the organization. However, these measures rely on employees being honest and able to accurately describe their feelings, which can sometimes be a limitation.

#### Impact of Job Satisfaction

Satisfied employees are more likely to stay with the company, perform well, and contribute positively. Dissatisfied employees might respond by leaving (exit), complaining (voice), staying loyal but unhappy (loyalty), or neglecting their work.


### 8. 🔄 Other Important Job Attitudes: Involvement, Commitment, and Beyond

#### Job Involvement

This refers to how much a person cares about and identifies with their job. Highly involved employees see their work as important and meaningful.

#### Psychological Empowerment

This is about employees’ beliefs in their ability to influence their work environment, their competence, the meaningfulness of their job, and their autonomy.

#### Organizational Commitment

Commitment means a strong emotional attachment to the organization, acceptance of its goals, willingness to work hard for it, and a desire to stay. It goes beyond just loyalty—it’s about truly believing in the organization’s values.

#### Perceived Organizational Support (POS)

POS is how much employees feel the organization values their contributions and cares about their well-being. When employees perceive strong support, they tend to show better citizenship behaviors, less tardiness, and improved customer service.

#### Employee Engagement

Engagement is the passion and enthusiasm employees have for their work. Engaged employees put energy and attention into their jobs, while disengaged employees may just “show up” without real involvement. Unfortunately, surveys show that only a minority of employees are highly engaged.

#### Organizational Citizenship Behaviour (OCB)

OCBs are voluntary, extra-role behaviors that employees do to help the organization, like helping coworkers or suggesting improvements. These behaviors improve morale, reduce supervision needs, and save costs. Managers who understand OCBs can encourage positive contributions while avoiding employee burnout.


### 9. 🔍 Are These Job Attitudes Really Different?

While job attitudes like satisfaction, involvement, and commitment are distinct concepts, they often overlap. This overlap can be due to the employee’s personality or the way these attitudes influence each other. Understanding these connections helps managers better support their employees and improve organizational outcomes.


### Summary

Understanding attitudes and job satisfaction is crucial for managing people effectively in organizations. Recognizing individuality helps managers appreciate the diverse backgrounds and traits employees bring. Attitudes shape how people think, feel, and behave at work, and they are influenced by personality, situations, and social context. Job satisfaction and other job attitudes like involvement, commitment, and engagement play a big role in how employees perform and stay motivated. Managers who understand these concepts can create better work environments, reduce conflict, and foster positive behaviors that benefit both employees and the organization.
