# Organizational Behaviour - Lesson 6: Fundamentals of Group Behaviour

## Groups and Group Classification
- A **group** is two or more interacting and interdependent individuals who come together to achieve particular objectives.
- **Formal groups** are defined by organizational structure, designated assignments, established tasks, and behaviours directed toward organizational goals.
- **Informal groups** are not formally structured or organizationally determined and mainly arise from social contact, but they can strongly influence behaviour and performance.

## Group Development
### Five-Stage Model
- **Forming** - members are polite and reserved, seek clarity about goals and tasks, and depend heavily on the leader.
- **Storming** - conflict, competing viewpoints, power struggles, leadership challenges, and emotional tension emerge.
- **Norming** - conflicts are resolved, common ground and norms develop, trust increases, and roles become clearer.
- **Performing** - the group becomes productive, coordinated, and increasingly self-managing.
- **Adjourning** - the group recognizes its achievements, members separate, and attention shifts to future activities.
- Groups do not always move neatly through the stages; they can progress at different speeds, remain in one stage, experience stages simultaneously, or regress.

### Punctuated-Equilibrium Model
- The **punctuated-equilibrium model** explains temporary groups working toward deadlines.
- The first meeting sets direction, followed by a period of inertia; at roughly the midpoint, awareness of time triggers major change; another period of activity follows, and the final meeting brings accelerated work.
- Temporary groups therefore experience **long periods of inertia interrupted by brief major changes**.

## Roles and Norms
- A **role** is a set of expected behaviour patterns associated with a position in a social unit.
- **Role perception** is how individuals understand the tasks, goals, and problems connected with their own role.
- **Role expectations** are the behaviours and responsibilities others expect from a person occupying a role.
- **Role conflict** occurs when a person faces incompatible roles or overlapping responsibilities and is associated with greater work-related stress.
- **Group norms** are shared standards of acceptable behaviour that state what members ought and ought not to do.
  - Norms can concern performance, appearance, social arrangements, resource allocation, communication, and decision-making.
- Norms differ from formal rules because they emerge socially and can influence behaviour with little external control.

## Sanctions and Workplace Deviance
- **Social sanctions** reinforce norms.
  - Positive sanctions include praise, recognition, increased status, and group benefits.
  - Negative sanctions include criticism, exclusion, and loss of trust or support.
- Sanctions promote conformity, reinforce cohesiveness, and help groups control behaviour.
- **Deviant workplace behaviour** is voluntary behaviour that violates important organizational norms and threatens the organization or its members.
  - **Production deviance** includes lateness, leaving early, intentionally working slowly, and withholding effort.
  - **Property deviance** includes theft, sabotage, vandalism, and misuse of equipment.
  - **Political deviance** includes favouritism, rumours, backstabbing, and blaming coworkers.
  - **Personal aggression** includes verbal abuse, sexual harassment, and physical assault.
- Causes include perceived unfairness, job dissatisfaction, poor culture, stress, weak leadership, peer influence, opportunity, rationalization, and individual differences.
- Prevention includes a positive culture, clear expectations, open communication, employee involvement, conflict-resolution mechanisms, support systems, training, recognition, monitoring, and leaders modelling appropriate behaviour.

## Status and Group Interaction
- **Status** is the socially defined position or rank given to a group or its members.
- Status commonly comes from **power over resources, contribution to group goals, and valued personal characteristics**.
- High-status members are usually given more freedom to deviate from norms and can resist conformity more easily.
- High-status members tend to speak, criticize, command, and interrupt more, while lower-status members often participate less.
- Groups lose useful expertise when lower-status members with relevant knowledge are not heard.

## Group Size and Social Loafing
- **Larger groups** are useful for obtaining diverse input and fact-finding, while **smaller groups of about seven members** are better at producing concrete outputs within deadlines.
- **Social loafing** or free riding is exerting less effort in a group than when working alone because individual accountability is reduced.
- Causes include diffusion of responsibility, low identifiability, low task importance, free riders, larger group size, and weak group culture.
- Social loafing appears more consistently in **individualist cultures** than in collectivist cultures.
- It can be reduced by common group goals, intergroup competition, peer evaluation, motivated members, and rewards linked partly to individual contributions.

## Cohesiveness and Diversity
- **Group cohesiveness** is the strength of the bonds and sense of unity connecting group members.
- Cohesiveness improves productivity only when **performance norms are also high**; high cohesiveness with low performance norms can produce low productivity.
- Cohesiveness can be increased by smaller groups, agreement on goals, more time together, higher group status, competition with other groups, group rewards, and physical isolation.
- **Group diversity** brings differences in age, gender, ethnicity, socioeconomic background, culture, and other perspectives.
- Diversity can improve creativity, insight, and problem-solving, but often increases early conflict and may reduce morale before benefits emerge.
- **Group faultlines** are hypothetical dividing lines that split a group into relatively homogeneous subgroups.
  - Strong faultlines can reduce trust, learning, creativity, and performance while increasing conflict and risky decisions.

## Group Decision Making
- Group decisions can provide **more information, more diverse viewpoints, and greater acceptance** of the final solution.
- Disadvantages include **slower decisions, conformity pressure, domination by a few members, and diluted responsibility**.

### Groupthink and Groupshift
- **Groupthink** occurs when pressure for conformity prevents critical evaluation of minority or unpopular views.
  - Symptoms include illusion of invulnerability, collective rationalization, belief in group morality, stereotyping outsiders, pressure on dissenters, self-censorship, illusion of unanimity, and mind guards.
  - It can be reduced through open dialogue, a devil's advocate, diverse teams, structured decision processes, anonymous feedback, independent thinking, breakout groups, and time for reflection.
- **Groupshift or group polarization** occurs when group discussion pushes members toward more extreme positions than they would take individually.
  - Causes include social comparison, cohesion, informational influence, desire for distinction, social loafing, and reduced accountability.

## Group Decision-Making Techniques
- **Interacting groups** meet face to face and use verbal and nonverbal interaction, often resolving disagreement through voting or another agreed decision rule.
- **Brainstorming** encourages members to generate many ideas without criticism during idea generation.
  - The slides note that individuals working alone may generate more ideas because simultaneous group discussion can block thought and sharing.
- **Nominal group technique** keeps members physically together but initially independent.
  - Members first generate ideas silently, present them without discussion, discuss and clarify all ideas, then independently rank them; the highest aggregate ranking determines the decision.
