# Organizational Behaviour - Lesson 7: Understanding Work Teams

## Teams and Work Groups
- A **team** is a group of individuals who work together interdependently toward a shared goal or mission.
- A work team creates **positive synergy through coordinated effort**, so collective performance can exceed the sum of individual inputs.
- A work team is a purposeful subset of a work group in which member interactions are deliberately interdependent.
- Work groups and work teams can both share information, generate ideas, pool resources, and coordinate activities, but they differ in emphasis:
  - **Work groups** focus mainly on information sharing, individual accountability, and individual work products, with neutral or sometimes negative synergy.
  - **Work teams** focus on collective performance, positive synergy, individual and mutual accountability, complementary skills, and collective work products.

## Types of Teams
- **Problem-solving teams** usually contain about five to ten members from the same department who meet regularly to address specific business problems.
- **Self-managed work teams** organize and manage their own work without traditional hierarchical supervision.
  - Fully self-managed teams may select members and evaluate one another, reducing the importance of conventional supervisory roles.
- **Cross-functional teams** bring employees from different work areas, usually at similar hierarchical levels, together to complete a task.
  - They support information exchange, new ideas, problem solving, and coordination of complex projects, but can require substantial coordination and longer development.
- **Virtual teams** collaborate through electronic communication rather than working in the same physical location.
  - Effective virtual teams require trust, close monitoring of progress, and visibility of team efforts within the organization.
  - Challenges include weaker social rapport, isolation, information-distribution problems, and difficulty finding the right amount of online communication.

## Multiteam Systems
- A **multiteam system** is two or more interdependent teams that work toward a higher shared goal, effectively forming a team of teams.
- Their success depends on coordination across otherwise independent teams.
- **Boundary spanners** coordinate with other subteams and can improve multiteam performance by reducing unnecessary communication and coordination demands.

## Team Effectiveness
- The Team Effectiveness Model should be treated as a **guide rather than a rigid rule**, because teams differ in form and structure.
- Teamwork should be used only when it is preferable to individual work.
- Team effectiveness includes **productivity, managers' evaluations of performance, and member satisfaction**.
- The model groups major influences into **context, team composition, and team processes**.

### Context
- **Adequate resources** include timely information, equipment, staffing, encouragement, and administrative support.
- **Leadership and structure** clarify responsibilities, workload, and coordination; shared leadership can be more effective than a traditional single-leader structure.
- **Climate of trust** encourages cooperation, reduces monitoring, supports risk-taking, and strengthens commitment to team goals and leadership.
- **Performance evaluation and reward systems** should recognize both individual contributions and positive team outcomes.

### Team Composition
- **Abilities of members** influence what the team can accomplish and how well it adapts to new problems.
- **Personality of members** matters; higher average conscientiousness and openness support performance, while highly disagreeable members can harm the team.
- **Role allocation** should ensure all required roles are filled, with capable and experienced members placed in central roles.
- **Diversity** can increase creativity and innovation by expanding knowledge and perspectives, but poor communication and conflict can increase turnover.
- **Team size** should be kept as small as the task allows; the slides identify about five to nine members as generally most effective.
- **Member preferences** matter because forcing people who strongly prefer individual work into teams can reduce morale and satisfaction.

### Team Processes
- **Common plan and purpose** provide direction; effective teams also show reflexivity by reviewing and adjusting their plans when conditions change.
- **Specific goals** translate the common purpose into measurable and realistic performance targets.
- **Team efficacy** is the shared belief that the team can succeed and can motivate members to work harder.
- **Shared mental models** are common understandings of how the team and its environment work.
  - Inconsistent mental models can create disputes about methods instead of focus on the task.
- **Social loafing** is reduced when members are both individually and jointly accountable.
- **Conflict** has different effects:
  - Relationship conflict based on personal incompatibility is generally dysfunctional.
  - Moderate task conflict can improve discussion, critical evaluation, creativity, and decisions during non-routine work, but very low or very high task conflict can reduce performance.

## Turning Individuals into Team Players
- Many employees are not naturally team players, and organizations may historically reward individual achievement.
- **Selection** should consider ability to fulfil team roles as well as technical requirements.
  - Openness, comfort with ambiguity, empathy, social orientation, and self-monitoring support cross-cultural team communication.
- **Training** can build problem-solving, communication, negotiation, conflict-management, and coaching skills.
- **Rewards** should encourage cooperation rather than only individual competition.
  - Recognition should include behaviours such as training colleagues, sharing information, resolving conflicts, and developing needed skills while still acknowledging individual contributions.

## When Teams Should Be Used
- Teams are not automatically the best structure for every task.
- Use a team when **more than one person can perform the work better**, especially when the work is complex and requires different perspectives.
- Use a team when the work creates a **shared purpose or collective goal** that is more than the sum of individual goals.
- Use a team when members are **interdependent**, meaning the success of each member depends on the success of the others and the success of the whole depends on each member.
